# Caplan RA: credit vs. pay, realistic pay, and the $255k grant — Research Report

Prepared 2026-10-18. Research only. No contact made, nothing drafted or sent.

## Q1 — Can you get BOTH course credit and pay for the same research work?

**Verdict: Your instinct is essentially right, but the rule is narrower than "no credit if you're paid." The hard, published ban is on double-counting the SAME work — and for USRA-funded work it is absolute. No single UAlberta-wide rule was found that says a directly grant-paid RA can never also hold a credit course.**

**What the authoritative sources actually say:**

- **NSERC USRA (the strict rule):** "Award holders should not receive academic credits for research work completed during the USRA work term" / "USRA is intended to be over and above the academic credits required for degree." This is NSERC's program rule, repeated on UBC, U of T, and Concordia USRA pages. Note: USRA is already closed for Winter 2027 anyway (deadline passed + full-time rule), so this rule doesn't directly govern a Caplan Winter 2027 placement — but it is the closest authoritative statement of the principle.
- **Alberta Innovates Summer Research Studentship (same principle):** "No academic course credit will be awarded for work funded by the Alberta Innovates Summer Research Studentship." (UCalgary page.)
- **UAlberta URI (no single published ban found):** UAlberta's "How to hire/register undergraduate researchers" page treats the tracks as separate categories with separate processes — employment (paid), scholarship/award, volunteer, and "research-based courses" (credit: "Enroll students following your department's established process for research-based courses"). It does **not** publish a sentence saying paid RA work can never also earn credit.

**The nuance — same work vs. separate work:**
- **Same hours/work counted twice (paid + credit): barred.** A credit course (PSYCH 299/396/398/496/498, CMPUT 499) requires its own syllabus, deliverables, and assessment. Re-labelling paid RA hours as credit coursework is double-counting, and it is what the USRA rule and general academic-integrity practice prohibit.
- **Paid on one project + credit on a genuinely separate project:** not barred by any published rule found in this research. But it would require the supervisor and department to agree to two distinct streams with separately assessable deliverables — and for one Winter 2027 placement in one lab, that is not a realistic setup. In practice, for the same placement: **pick one — paid OR credit.**

**Sources:** UBC USRA page (https://facultystaff.students.ubc.ca/student-affairs/ubc-career-centre/undergraduate-student-research-awards) · U of T USRA page (https://undergrad.engineering.utoronto.ca/experiential-learning/research-opportunities/nserc-usra/) · Alberta Innovates SRS (https://signaturelearning.ucalgary.ca/undergraduate-research/urex-summer/alberta-innovates-summer-research-studentships) · UAlberta URI hire page (https://www.ualberta.ca/en/current-students/undergraduate-research-initiative/hire-undergraduate-researchers.html)

## Q2 — Realistic pay expectation for a UAlberta undergrad RA paid from a professor's NSERC grant, Winter 2027

**Verdict: UAlberta publishes NO official "undergrad RA hourly rate." The best UAlberta-published anchor implies ≈ $17.86/hr. Realistic planning band: $17–$20/hr, central figure $18/hr. For a Winter term at 12 hrs/week (URI's recommended max during term), expect roughly $3,300–$3,800 gross for the term.**

**What the sources actually say:**

- **UAlberta URI budgeting guidance (strongest anchor):** "for a typical summer student position (e.g. full-time at 35 hours/week for 16 weeks), we recommend budgeting approximately $10,000 per student." $10,000 ÷ (35 × 16 = 560 hrs) ≈ **$17.86/hr** implied. Same page: "For part-time positions during the academic year… we recommend a maximum average of **12 hours/week** during the academic year."
- **UAlberta HR minimums (floor, not typical):** Excluded student employees: at least Alberta minimum wage **$15.00/hr** + mandatory employment-related costs. Non-excluded positions: at least Grade 1, Step 1 on the NASA salary scale (current dollar figure **not verified** in this research — not stated here).
- **USRA floor (benchmark only):** $6,000 (NSERC) + minimum $2,400 supervisor top-up = $8,400 for 16 weeks at 35 hrs/week → **$15/hr** floor. USRA is closed for Winter 2027, so this is a comparison point only.
- **Alberta general minimum wage:** $15.00/hr (unchanged since Oct 1, 2018; no increase on Oct 1, 2026).
- **Third-party market data (weak):** Indeed lists Edmonton "student researcher" at ~$17.01/hr average (6 salaries). Treat as corroboration only, not a source of truth.

**Term totals at the $17–$20/hr band (16 weeks, gross pay to the student):**

| hrs/week | Hours (16 wks) | At $17/hr | At $18/hr | At $20/hr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 160 | $2,720 | $2,880 | $3,200 |
| 12 (URI recommended max) | 192 | $3,264 | $3,456 | $3,840 |
| 15 | 240 | $4,080 | $4,320 | $4,800 |
| 20 | 320 | $5,440 | $5,760 | $6,400 |

**What pushes it up or down:**
- **Up:** non-excluded classification on the NASA scale; a supervisor who budgets at the URI $10k/summer equivalent; more hours (but see below).
- **Down:** excluded-student classification at/near the $15/hr floor; a tight grant year.
- **Hours cap matters more than rate:** URI recommends a **maximum average of 12 hrs/week** during the academic year. 20 hrs/week is above the recommended max and may simply not be offered alongside coursework. Do not budget on 20 hrs/week.
- **Employer costs are on top, not deducted from your hourly:** mandatory employment-related costs (CPP/EI, vacation pay) are paid by the grant on top of the hourly rate — they raise the cost to the supervisor (~10–15%) but do not reduce the student's gross hourly pay.

**Sources:** UAlberta URI hire page (https://www.ualberta.ca/en/current-students/undergraduate-research-initiative/hire-undergraduate-researchers.html) · UAlberta USRA page (https://www.ualberta.ca/en/current-students/undergraduate-research-initiative/funding/usra.html) · Alberta minimum wage (https://www.recordinglaw.com/canada/employment-law/minimum-wage-alberta/)

## Q3 — Is the grant "a lot," and is it "all used up by now"?

**Verdict: Two corrections. (1) The figure is $255,000 total, not $245,000 (per Caplan's CV, Oct 17, 2026). (2) "All used up by now" is wrong as a matter of mechanics — NSERC Discovery Grants arrive in ANNUAL instalments, and unspent balances carry within the grant period. But $51k/yr is modest, not large: one PhD student's stipend eats roughly half of it. Whether there is room for an undergrad RA in Winter 2027 depends on how much of the current year's allocation is already committed — only Caplan knows.**

**How the money actually flows (NSERC sources):**
- **Annual instalments, not a lump sum.** NSERC terms: "You will receive the subsequent instalment of your grant on or near the anniversary of the start date" (subject to funds available and demonstrated need; NSERC may reduce/defer/cancel an instalment if need isn't demonstrated). For Caplan's grant (start Apr 1, 2023), instalments arrive ~each April.
- **Unspent funds carry within the grant period.** The Tri-agency Guide on Financial Administration discusses "build-up of funds" in a grant account as a normal occurrence (with deferral of the next instalment if the build-up means it isn't needed) — i.e., unspent money stays in the account and rolls forward year to year. It is only at the END of the grant (plus any extension) that residual balances are transferred to the institution's General Research Fund or returned.
- **Discovery Grants are "grants in aid," not full-cost funding.** NSERC's program page: Discovery Grants "provide long-term operating funds… but are not meant to support the full costs of a research program."
- **Which instalment covers Winter 2027:** Jan–Mar 2027 falls in grant Year 4 (Apr 2026–Mar 2027, instalment received ~Apr 2026); Apr 2027 falls in Year 5 (Apr 2027–Mar 2028, instalment arriving ~Apr 2027). So Winter 2027 is paid mostly from the Year-4 instalment that already arrived, plus the Year-5 instalment for its final month.

**Why ~$51k/yr is modest — a realistic annual budget sketch (grad stipends from published UAlberta sources; other lines labelled typical/approximate):**

| Expense | Typical annual cost | Note |
|---|---|---|
| One PhD student stipend | ~$25,000 (published UAlberta minimums: Pediatrics PhD $25,197; GPS PhD minimum $25k in year 1, $100k over 4 years) | One student ≈ half the annual grant |
| One MSc student stipend | ~$24,000 (published: Pediatrics MSc $24,089; AFNS MSc minimum $24,089) | Two grad students ≈ the whole grant — in practice grants usually cover part of stipends, topped by other funding |
| Participant payments | ~$2,000–$5,000/yr (typical/approximate) | Psych experiments pay participants per session |
| Supplies, software, equipment | ~$2,000–$5,000/yr (typical/approximate) | |
| Conference travel | ~$3,000–$6,000/yr (typical/approximate) | ~$2–3k per conference trip |
| Open-access publication fees | ~$2,000–$4,000 per article (typical/approximate) | |
| **An undergrad RA at ~$3,500–$4,500/term** | **~7–9% of one year's $51k** | Affordable IF the year's allocation isn't already committed |

**Bottom line on Q3:** The $255k was never sitting in a pile to be "used up" — it arrives ~$51k/year, and each year's money is spent as it comes. The right question is not "is the grant gone?" but "is there uncommitted room in the Year-4 (Apr 2026–Mar 2027) allocation?" That is a question only Caplan can answer.
